Beginner guide · Bokmål

Norwegian for beginners: start with the basics

You do not need to memorise an entire grammar book before you begin speaking. Start with a small set of useful expressions, learn a few reliable sentence patterns, and practise them in context.

This guide introduces Norwegian Bokmål, one of Norway's two official written standards. People also speak many regional dialects, so you will hear differences in pronunciation and word choice. The examples below are introductory study examples rather than a complete course.

Suggested routine: spend five to ten focused minutes per day on words you can reuse. Say the examples out loud, write one sentence about your own life, then review yesterday's words.

1. Learn a few Norwegian expressions first

2. Understand basic sentence order

A common Norwegian main-clause pattern is subject + verb + the rest of the sentence. The verb generally occupies the second position in a main clause, often called the V2 rule. That second position can be the first phrase after the subject, not necessarily the second word in every sentence.

NorwegianLiteral structureNatural English
Jeg lærer norsk.Jeg (I) + lærer (learn) + norsk (Norwegian).I am learning Norwegian.
Jeg bor i Norge.Jeg (I) + bor (live) + i Norge (in Norway).I live in Norway.
I dag jobber jeg.I dag (today) + jobber (work) + jeg (I).Today, I am working.
Snakker du norsk?Snakker (speak) + du (you) + norsk (Norwegian)?Do you speak Norwegian?

Notice the word order in I dag jobber jeg: because I dag comes first, the verb jobber comes next, followed by jeg. Questions can also change the order. Treat these as useful starting patterns and learn more as you progress.

3. Get familiar with nouns and articles

Norwegian nouns have grammatical gender. In Bokmål, common patterns include masculine, feminine and neuter nouns; feminine nouns can in many contexts also be treated with masculine forms in Bokmål. Beginners should learn the article and the noun together instead of memorising the noun alone.

Indefinite formMeaningDefinite form
en bila carbilen — the car
ei bok / en boka bookboka / boken — the book
et husa househuset — the house

These are examples, not a complete list of noun rules. Use a learner dictionary when you need to confirm the gender or definite form of a new noun.

4. Practise the letters æ, ø and å

Modern Norwegian uses the Latin alphabet plus three additional letters: æ, ø and å. These are separate letters, not decorative accents. English does not have exact equivalents for every Norwegian vowel, so descriptions are only approximations.

  • Æ / æ: somewhat like the vowel in English “bad” for many speakers, but the sound depends on the word and accent.
  • Ø / ø: try shaping your lips for “oo” while making a vowel closer to “e”.
  • Å / å: often resembles the vowel in English “saw” for many English accents, though the exact sound varies.

Try the audio examples in the Norwegian pronunciation guide. Browser voices vary, so also listen to trusted native-speaker recordings and real conversations.

5. A practical 7-day beginner plan

  1. Day 1 — Greetings: learn Hei, God morgen, Ha det and Takk. Say each one aloud.
  2. Day 2 — Introductions: practise “Jeg heter …” and “Jeg lærer norsk.” Add your own name and details.
  3. Day 3 — Everyday verbs: study være (to be), ha (to have), bo (to live), snakke (to speak) and lære (to learn).
  4. Day 4 — Food and drink: learn words for water, coffee, bread and common ways to ask politely for something.
  5. Day 5 — Places and directions: practise asking where a station, shop or bus stop is.
  6. Day 6 — Listen and repeat: use short recordings, repeat the sentence, and pay attention to rhythm rather than aiming for a perfect accent.
  7. Day 7 — Review: revisit difficult words, complete a short quiz and write five sentences about yourself.

6. How to remember vocabulary for longer

Recall is stronger when you try to remember an answer before seeing it. Use flashcards in both directions when possible, put new words inside short sentences, and return to difficult words on later days. Avoid marking a word as known just because it looks familiar.

Common questions

How long does it take to learn basic Norwegian?

It depends on your previous language experience, study time, exposure and goals. A consistent short daily routine can help you build a useful foundation, but there is no guaranteed timeline.

Is Norwegian pronunciation difficult for English speakers?

Some sounds and pitch patterns may be unfamiliar, and regional dialects vary. Start by listening to standard examples, repeating short phrases and asking for feedback rather than trying to imitate every dialect at once.

Can I use Bokmål in daily life in Norway?

Bokmål is a written standard used widely in Norway, but people speak regional dialects. Spoken language may differ from written examples. Learning Bokmål is a useful start, not a substitute for listening to everyday speech.
